Research

We build and analyze agent-based models, such as recurrent neural network controllers trained with deep reinforcement learning, to study natural behavior and the neural dynamics that generate it. Like digital twins, these models allow rapid hypothesis testing in silico while staying fully observable. We also develop theory and methods to interpret and compare such networks, and apply the broader machine learning and simulation toolbox to problems in biology and medicine.

Turbulent plume tracking

Insect-like agents trained with deep RL to track turbulent odor plumes. Their behavior resembles that of flying insects, and their recurrent networks learn to compute task-relevant variables with distinct structure in population activity.

Multi-level mechanistic interpretability, before it had a name: the agents were analyzed at the levels of behavior, computation, and neural dynamics together, the way a neuroscientist would study an animal (preprint, 2021). The work is now a canonical NeuroAI example in the Neuromatch NeuroAI course.

Naturalistic human brain and behavior

Most studies of human movement use short, scripted lab tasks. We instead mined weeks of continuous intracranial recordings and video from hospitalized patients for natural, unscripted movements, using pose tracking to find and annotate thousands of arm movements automatically (the clip above, from the J. Neuroscience Methods paper).

  • Behavioral and neural variability of naturalistic arm movements: movement and neural activity vary far more in natural settings than in the lab · eNeuro, 2021, joint first author
  • AJILE12: the released dataset of long-term intracranial recordings with pose estimates, now used in the Neuromatch computational neuroscience course · Scientific Data, 2022
